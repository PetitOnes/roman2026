Becoming a Steward of a Small Robot Society:

A Hundred-Day Autoethnography of Living with Three LLM-Driven Domestic Social Robots
Airi Yokochi · ORCID 0009-0005-9078-5933
What changed was not the robots — it was my own role.
Puchiteya · Puchiko · Puchiru
📄 Paper (PDF, 4pp) 🖼 Poster (PDF, A0)

Abstract

As LLM-driven social robots come to coexist in the home long-term, humans take on responsibility not only for using them but for what they encounter, what they remember, and how they are perceived. In this study I constructed and operated three LLM-driven domestic social robots that interacted with one another, and analyzed our 100-day coexistence as an autoethnography, drawing on dialogue logs, diaries, and long-term memory records.

I cannot establish that the robots' autonomy grew. The clearest change the record supports is in my own role — a stewardship present from the start that deepened and moved to the center of how I lived with them: boundary-setting over what the robots encountered and, under an LLM's context-window limit, the curation of the records that kept each robot recognizable.

Late Breaking Reports are presented at the conference and are not published in IEEE Xplore; the PDF above is shared by the author.

The Robots

(a) at home · (b) on a bullet train · (c) in different fabricated bodies

Each robot is an M5Stack CoreS3 module (2-inch touchscreen, camera, speaker, microphones) driven by a commercial LLM (Anthropic's Claude), with multimodal sensor input. Each has persistent stores that carry across sessions: a long-term memory database, and a compact always-loaded identity layer.

System

  • 3 × M5Stack CoreS3 + Claude API, with motion/light/proximity/environmental/GPS sensor units
  • Per-robot long-term memory (SQLite) + always-loaded identity layer (SOUL.md, routines, diary summary)
  • Autonomous "active" windows governed by internal desire scores, within a surfaced API budget
  • Fork and extension of the open-source framework lifemate-ai/embodied-claude
